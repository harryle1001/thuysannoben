Every year towards the end of the year, when the northeast monsoon arrives, nights turn cold and out-of-season rain falls, many farming areas again report ponds with white spot. The article on [white spot disease (WSSV)](/bai-viet/benh-dom-trang-wssv-tren-tom) covers the signs and general prevention. This article answers three specific questions: **why white spot breaks out more easily in cold weather**, **where the pathogen enters the pond from**, and **how to report and handle a diseased pond in line with regulations**.

## Water temperature and white spot disease

The WOAH (World Organisation for Animal Health) Manual of Diagnostic Tests (2023) notes that water temperature has a very strong influence on whether the disease develops:

- Average water temperatures **of 18–30°C** are the range favourable for white spot outbreaks.
- In challenge experiments, WSSV mortality **decreased when temperature rose above 32°C**.
- Sudden salinity changes and other stressors can trigger the disease.

The disease prevention and control guidance of the Department of Agriculture and Environment of Quang Nam (2025) is more specific to Vietnamese conditions: the disease occurs year-round but is strongest during **seasonal transitions** (late spring to early summer, late autumn to early winter, the change between rainy and dry seasons), when **water temperature is below 26°C**, the environment fluctuates and shrimp resistance declines. Shrimp are most susceptible at **40–65 days after stocking**; the disease can kill up to 90% within 3–7 days.

In other words, cold does not create the virus by itself. But if the virus is already in the pond or in the shrimp, cold and fluctuating water will make the disease more likely to break out.

## Where does the white spot pathogen enter the pond from?

### Crustaceans and wild animals

According to WOAH, many wild decapod crustacean species are **reservoirs** of the virus, including various crabs, fiddler crabs, small shrimp, krill and snapping shrimp. Some small species such as copepods, rotifers and Artemia can carry the virus without showing disease. Polychaete worms and some copepods have been identified as vectors. Molluscs and aquatic insects can carry the virus mechanically.

The Quang Nam guidance lists pathogen carriers including decapod crustaceans, polychaete worms, bivalve molluscs and squid. The disease is transmitted horizontally from diseased shrimp, intermediate hosts and virus-contaminated fresh feed, and vertically from broodstock to offspring.

### Postlarvae and intake water

Shrimp that look healthy can still carry and transmit the virus. Dead and weak shrimp in the pond are an important source of infection. Untreated intake water from a shared canal, especially when there are diseased ponds in the surrounding area, is a route that brings the virus in very quickly.

### How long can the virus survive outside shrimp?

WOAH summarises: in the laboratory, the virus survives at least 30 days in seawater at 30°C and at least 3–4 days in pond water. In pond simulations, virus in bottom sludge **lost infectivity after 21 days of sun-drying**, or after 40 days if the sludge stayed submerged. That is why a diseased pond must be dried and prepared thoroughly before restocking.

## Preventing white spot in the cold season

- **Avoid stocking in the cold season** is a WOAH recommendation. Follow the local crop calendar and weather forecasts before stocking.
- **Disease-free postlarvae**: use specific pathogen free (SPF) postlarvae or postlarvae with a PCR-negative result for WSSV.
- **Block crustaceans**: take water through filter bags, put crab-proof netting around the pond banks, eliminate crustaceans in the settling pond, and scare birds away.
- **Treat water** in the settling pond before supplying it. WOAH notes that chlorine at 100 ppm for 10 minutes or iodophor at 100 ppm for 10 minutes inactivated the virus under experimental conditions.
- **Keep the environment stable** when the weather turns cold and rainy: monitor temperature, pH, alkalinity and salinity every day, and avoid large water exchanges that cause sudden changes in salinity or temperature. See also how to keep [pH and alkalinity stable](/bai-viet/quan-ly-ph-do-kiem-ao-nuoi-tom).
- **Feed according to actual appetite** as checked on feeding trays, avoiding uneaten feed that fouls the bottom.
- **Boost resistance**: WOAH notes some reports showing that beta-glucan, vitamin C and seaweed extracts may improve shrimp resistance to WSSV. These are supporting measures and cannot replace biosecurity.
- **Check shrimp daily**: activity, colour, shell, hepatopancreas, gut and feed intake. Shrimp that stop eating, swim lethargically, gather at the pond edge or have white spots of 0.5–2 mm under the carapace are a sign that samples must be taken immediately.

## Sampling for WSSV testing

According to the Quang Nam guidance:

- Samples must be live or moribund shrimp. Live shrimp can be kept at 4°C for 24 hours.
- For large shrimp, take about 3–5 g of gill tissue and preserve in 90% alcohol, at a sample-to-alcohol ratio of 1/10.
- For postlarvae, take about 30 animals per sample and preserve in 90% alcohol.
- Test by PCR or real-time PCR according to veterinary authority guidance.

## When a pond has white spot: reporting and handling in line with regulations

White spot disease is on the **list of aquatic animal diseases for which an outbreak must be officially declared** under Circular 04/2016/TT-BNNPTNT (consolidated document No. 30/VBHN-BNNPTNT of 2024). Under Article 5 of this document, anyone who discovers aquatic animals that are diseased, dying in large numbers or showing abnormal signs must report to the commune veterinary officer, the commune People's Committee or the nearest veterinary authority.

The Quang Nam guidance sets out the steps to take:

1. **Report** to the nearest aquatic animal health authority and follow its technical guidance.
2. **Notify surrounding farms** so that everyone can take preventive action and avoid widespread transmission.
3. Shrimp that have reached marketable size may be harvested in accordance with regulations. For shrimp below marketable size, **do not dump diseased or dead shrimp into the environment**; destroy them in accordance with regulations.
4. **Water from the diseased pond** must be treated with chlorine at 30 ppm or an equivalent approved chemical, and may only be discharged **after 5 days**.
5. Bottom sludge, pond banks, equipment and vehicles used for diseased shrimp must be treated, disinfected and sanitised.
6. Take measures to stop birds and intermediate animals from carrying the pathogen from the diseased pond to other ponds.

White spot disease **has no treatment**. Farmers should carefully question any advertisement claiming to cure white spot.

After a disease crop, according to recommendations published by the National Agricultural Extension Centre (2015), the pond should be dried and prepared for about 1.5–2 months to cut off the source of disease, rather than restocking straight away.

## Frequently asked questions

### At what water temperature are shrimp most prone to white spot?

WOAH notes that average temperatures of 18–30°C favour outbreaks. Guidance in Vietnam highlights a high risk when water temperature falls below 26°C, especially during seasonal transitions.

### Can crabs in the pond transmit white spot to shrimp?

They can. Many wild crabs, fiddler crabs and small shrimp are reservoirs of white spot virus. Filter intake water, put netting around the pond banks and eliminate crustaceans before stocking.

### Is it mandatory to report a pond with white spot?

Yes. White spot is on the list of diseases for which an outbreak must be officially declared. When shrimp die in large numbers or disease is suspected, the pond owner must report to the commune veterinary officer or the nearest veterinary authority.

### How should water from a white spot pond be treated before discharge?

According to the Quang Nam guidance, treat it with chlorine at 30 ppm or an equivalent approved chemical, and discharge it into the environment only after 5 days.
