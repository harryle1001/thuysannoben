Minerals are the shrimp's "shell-building material". At every moult, shrimp must take up large amounts of calcium, magnesium and potassium from the water and feed to form a new shell. Ponds that are short of minerals or have an unbalanced mineral profile will cause **soft shell, incomplete moulting, bent body, muscle necrosis (white muscle) and slow growth**. Applying minerals correctly helps shrimp moult evenly, harden their shells quickly and gain weight well, especially in high-density lined ponds and low-salinity brackish ponds.

## Which minerals do shrimp need?

### Macro minerals

- **Calcium (Ca)**: the main component of the shell.
- **Magnesium (Mg)**: helps form the shell and supports moulting enzymes and neuromuscular function.
- **Potassium (K) and sodium (Na)**: regulate osmotic pressure, especially right after moulting; potassium-deficient shrimp are prone to bent body and muscle necrosis.
- **Bicarbonate (alkalinity)**: the carbonate source for shell formation and for keeping pH stable.

### Trace minerals

Iron, zinc, copper, manganese, cobalt, selenium and others are needed in small amounts but take part in enzymes, immunity and blood formation.

### Mineral ratios matter more than the total amount

In natural seawater, **magnesium is about 3 times calcium** and calcium is roughly equal to potassium. Diluted brackish ponds, freshened ponds or ponds after rain are not only short of minerals but also have skewed ratios, particularly a shortage of magnesium and potassium. It is therefore better to use pre-blended minerals containing all components rather than applying lime alone.

## Pond-applied and feed-mixed minerals: when to use each?

### Minerals applied to the pond

These add dissolved minerals to the water, which shrimp absorb through the gills and shell, especially during moulting. They are suitable for maintaining alkalinity, replenishing minerals after rain and stimulating even moulting. Examples:

- [MIX 79](/san-pham/mix-79), [MIRAL N8](/san-pham/miral-n8): alkalinity-raising, shell-hardening minerals; routine dose **5 kg per 3,000 m³ of water, every 2 days**; when alkalinity needs raising, use 5 kg per 1,500 m³ for 2 consecutive days.
- [KALIMIX](/san-pham/kalimix): shell-hardening mineral for lined ponds; routine dose **3 kg per 3,000 m³, every 2 days**.
- [RIMIX](/san-pham/rimix): alkalinity-raising, shell-hardening mineral for lined ponds; routine dose 5 kg per 3,000 m³, every 2 days.
- [PREMIX MINERAL](/san-pham/premix-khoang-khoang-kich-lot): mineral that stimulates moulting and rapid shell hardening.

### Minerals mixed into feed

Liquid minerals that are easily absorbed, giving shrimp minerals directly through the gut; useful when shrimp are stunted or show bent body or muscle necrosis:

- [CALCIPHO](/san-pham/calcipho): mix **10 ml/kg of feed**, feed 2–3 times/day throughout the crop.
- [CAPHOTE](/san-pham/caphote): mix **5 ml/kg of feed** daily to prevent bent body and muscle necrosis.

High-density ponds should **combine both**: pond application to maintain the baseline minerals in the water, and feed mixing to supplement directly.

## The best time to apply minerals

- **At night, around 20:00–22:00**: shrimp moult mainly at night, so minerals applied at this time are absorbed best.
- **On days of mass moulting**: usually around the spring tides (the beginning and middle of the lunar month), when many moults are seen in the feeding trays.
- **After heavy rain**: rain dilutes minerals and alkalinity, so replenish them immediately.
- **2–3 days before harvest**: helps shrimp harden their shells and look good.
- Do not apply minerals at the same time as disinfectants or algicides; space them at least half a day apart.

## Mineral doses by stage

- **First month**: shrimp are small; use a low routine dose and focus on keeping alkalinity stable.
- **From the second month**: shrimp moult frequently; increase the frequency of pond mineral application according to the routine dose on the label, combined with feed-mixed minerals.
- **High-density lined ponds, ponds with salinity below 10 ppt**: mineral demand is higher; monitor alkalinity and shell condition to increase the frequency.

Specific doses always follow the product label; if soft shell persists, call a technician to have the dose calculated based on pond volume and salinity.

## Common mistakes when applying minerals

- **Applying lime only** without adding magnesium and potassium, which unbalances the mineral profile.
- Applying minerals under the midday sun, when high pH makes the minerals precipitate and the shrimp cannot absorb them.
- Applying too much at once instead of spreading it evenly on a routine schedule.
- Not measuring alkalinity, so you do not know whether the pond is deficient or sufficient.
- Skipping feed-mixed minerals once shrimp already show bent body or muscle necrosis.

## Frequently asked questions

### How often should minerals be applied for shrimp?

For pond-applied minerals, the routine dose is usually every 2 days according to the labels of Noben mineral products. Feed-mixed minerals are used daily, at 2–3 feedings.

### Should minerals be applied during the day or at night?

At night, around 20:00–22:00, is best, because shrimp moult mostly at night and pH is lower at night, so minerals precipitate less.

### What alkalinity is sufficient for whiteleg shrimp?

Brackish ponds should keep alkalinity at 120–180 mg/L; low-salinity ponds should keep it at the upper end of this range.

### Are bent body and muscle necrosis caused by mineral deficiency?

They are often related to mineral deficiency, especially of potassium and magnesium, combined with temperature shock and pH shock. Supplement both pond-applied and feed-mixed minerals while stabilising the environment.
