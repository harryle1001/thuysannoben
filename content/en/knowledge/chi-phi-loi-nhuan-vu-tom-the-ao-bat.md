"How much does it cost to raise one kilo of whiteleg shrimp, and what price do I need to sell at to make a profit?" is the question farmers ask most when feed prices rise. This article compiles the **costs of farming whiteleg shrimp in lined ponds** from published studies, then shows how to calculate the **cost per kg of shrimp**, the **break-even point** and ways to reduce costs yourself.

All figures in the article state their source and survey period; prices vary by year and by region, so use them only as benchmarks for comparison.

## Real-world data: how much does a whiteleg shrimp crop in a lined pond cost?

**Survey of lined ponds in the Mekong Delta (2020).** Huynh Van Hien et al. (Vietnam Journal of Agricultural Science and Technology, 2021) interviewed 178 households in Soc Trang, Bac Lieu, Ca Mau and Kien Giang. Average results:

- Stocking density 157.1 shrimp/m², culture period 90–100 days, harvest size 61.6 shrimp/kg, FCR 1.3.
- Yield 15.9 tonnes/ha/crop; total cost about VND 1.29 billion/ha/crop.
- **Production cost VND 81,100/kg** (lowest in Soc Trang at VND 77,600/kg, highest in Kien Giang at VND 87,900/kg).
- Selling price VND 116,700/kg, profit VND 35,600/kg, profit margin 44%.

**Survey of super-intensive lined above-ground tanks in Ca Mau (2022–2023).** Pham Tien Si et al. (Can Tho University Journal of Science, 2024), 60 households: final survival 75.3%, eFCR 1.29, yield 4.18 tonnes/1,000 m³/crop. Total cost VND 335.6 million/1,000 m³/crop, **production cost VND 80,755/kg**, average selling price VND 142,481/kg.

**Price context in early 2026.** According to Tuoi Tre (9 March 2026), in An Giang whiteleg shrimp at size 100 shrimp/kg fetched about VND 90,000/kg, and farmers considered that the price had to be above VND 100,000/kg to make a decent profit; feed cost VND 560,000–900,000 per 20–25 kg bag.

## Cost structure: feed always comes first

Share of each cost item (% of total cost) in the two sources with detailed data; CTU-RAS is the recirculating system developed by Can Tho University and deployed in Ca Mau (Le Quoc Viet et al., report of August 2026):

| Cost item | Mekong Delta lined ponds, 2020 (Hien et al., 2021) | CTU-RAS Ca Mau (report of August 2026) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Feed | 53.8% | 52.8% (plus pumpkin 1.8%) |
| Postlarvae | 10.5% | 10.9% |
| Drugs, chemicals | 9.3% | 8.7% |
| Electricity, fuel | 7.2% | 9.3% |
| Labour | 2.0% (hired labour) | 8.0% |
| Depreciation | 13.8% | 8.4% |
| Other (pond preparation, loan interest) | 3.6% | – |

In the Ca Mau above-ground tanks (Si et al., 2024), feed accounted for 63.92% of **variable costs**, and drugs and chemicals 16.00%.

Common ground: **feed accounts for roughly half to nearly two-thirds of costs**.

### Depreciation of liners and infrastructure

Investment in lined ponds has to be spread (depreciated) across each crop:

- According to Hien et al. (2021), fixed costs accounted for 13.8% of total costs: depreciation of construction works 7.0%; machinery, equipment and liners 5.7%; storehouses and other materials 1.1%.
- Si et al. (2024) calculated depreciation by service life: pond excavation and tank construction 10 years; liners 5 years; equipment (air diffusers, paddlewheel aerators, motors, pumps) 5 years; generators 15 years. Of these, liner depreciation was the highest, at about VND 2.39 million/1,000 m³/crop.

Depreciation per crop = **purchase price ÷ years of service ÷ crops per year**. Forgetting this item makes you think you are earning more than you really are.

## How to calculate the cost per kg of shrimp from FCR and survival

Three formulas farmers need to know:

1. **Yield (kg) = number of shrimp stocked × survival rate ÷ harvest size (shrimp/kg).**
2. **FCR = total kg of feed fed ÷ kg of shrimp harvested.** (Boyd, 2021).
3. **Production cost (VND/kg) = total cost for the whole crop ÷ total kg of shrimp harvested.**

From this it follows that **feed cost per kg of shrimp = FCR × price of 1 kg of feed**. Meanwhile PL, electricity and depreciation are almost fixed for a crop, so **low survival reduces yield and pushes up the production cost**.

The CTU-RAS data (2026) illustrate this: sites 1 and 2 had survival of 74.3% and 68.9% and production costs of VND 76,000 and VND 69,500/kg; site 3 had survival of only 42.1%, FCR 1.35, and its production cost jumped to VND 107,200/kg.

### Hypothetical example to illustrate the calculation

*The figures below are a hypothetical example to illustrate the calculation, not actual data from any pond.*

Assumptions: a 1,000 m² lined pond, 150,000 shrimp stocked, harvest size 60 shrimp/kg, feed price VND 30,000/kg, and costs other than feed (PL, electricity, chemicals, probiotics, labour, depreciation) totalling VND 62 million/crop.

| Hypothetical scenario | Survival | FCR | Yield | Feed cost | Total cost | Production cost |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A | 75% | 1.3 | 1,875 kg | VND 73.1 million | VND 135.1 million | ~VND 72,100/kg |
| B (poor pond) | 60% | 1.5 | 1,500 kg | VND 67.5 million | VND 129.5 million | ~VND 86,300/kg |
| C (good pond) | 80% | 1.2 | 2,000 kg | VND 72.0 million | VND 134.0 million | VND 67,000/kg |

Scenario A: 150,000 × 75% ÷ 60 = 1,875 kg; feed 1,875 × 1.3 × VND 30,000 ≈ VND 73.1 million; production cost (73.1 + 62) million ÷ 1,875 kg ≈ VND 72,100/kg.

Total cost is almost unchanged, but the poor pond (B) has a production cost nearly VND 20,000 per kilo higher than the good pond (C).

## Break-even point: what price do you need to avoid a loss?

- **Break-even selling price = production cost.** Selling below the production cost means a loss, regardless of whether the yield is large or small.
- **Break-even yield = total cost ÷ expected selling price.**

Continuing hypothetical example A, with an assumed selling price of VND 90,000/kg: break-even yield = VND 135.1 million ÷ 90,000 ≈ 1,500 kg. If the pond harvests 1,875 kg, the profit is about 1,875 × 90,000 − VND 135.1 million ≈ VND 33.6 million. But in scenario B (production cost ~VND 86,300/kg), at the same selling price of VND 90,000/kg the profit is only about VND 5.5 million, a very thin margin.

## How to reduce the cost per kg of shrimp

**1. Lower the FCR, because feed is the largest cost.**
- Feed according to feeding trays, split into multiple meals, and reduce feeding in bad weather and when shrimp are moulting. See [feed management for shrimp](/bai-viet/quan-ly-thuc-an-cho-tom-an-dung-cach).
- Boyd (2021) points out that a low FCR not only reduces feed cost but also reduces waste and oxygen demand: in his estimates table, oxygen demand is about 1,100 kg per tonne of product at FCR 1.0, rising to 2,200 kg/tonne at FCR 2.0.
- Keep the gut healthy so shrimp absorb feed well; see the [digestive enzymes and immunity solution](/giai-phap/men-tieu-hoa-tang-de-khang).

**2. Keep survival high.** PL is a fixed cost; every shrimp lost means the money spent on PL and the feed already fed are lost too. Choose disease-free PL ([how to choose good whiteleg shrimp postlarvae and check PCR results](/bai-viet/cach-chon-tom-giong-the-tot-kiem-tra-pcr)), prepare the pond thoroughly, and monitor shrimp health following the [care schedule by days of culture](/lich-cham-soc).

**3. Use electricity sensibly.** Electricity and fuel account for about 7–9% of total costs; increase aerator running time gradually in line with the amount of feed rather than running at maximum throughout the crop, but never switch aerators off at night or before dawn; always keep dissolved oxygen ≥ 4 mg/L (ideally ≥ 5 mg/L). See [paddlewheel aerator layout and dissolved oxygen in shrimp ponds](/bai-viet/bo-tri-quat-nuoc-oxy-hoa-tan-ao-tom).

**4. Use drugs and probiotics at the right dose and the right time.** Drugs and chemicals account for about 9% (Hien et al., 2021) to 16% of variable costs (Si et al., 2024). Test the water before treating, calculate the dose from the actual volume with the [dose calculator](/tinh-lieu), and avoid adding extra "just to be safe".

**5. Reduce the cost of capital.** Buying feed on deferred payment adds 10–15% (Tuoi Tre, 2026).

**6. Make liners and aerators last** to reduce depreciation per crop. See also [whiteleg shrimp farming techniques in lined ponds](/bai-viet/ky-thuat-nuoi-tom-the-ao-lot-bat-mat-do-cao).

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the current production cost of whiteleg shrimp in lined ponds?

About VND 81,100/kg (Mekong Delta lined ponds, 2020 survey), VND 80,755/kg (Ca Mau above-ground tanks, 2022–2023) and VND 69,500–107,200/kg (CTU-RAS, 2026 report). Farmers should calculate their own figures from their pond records.

### What percentage of shrimp farming costs is feed?

About 53.8% of total costs in the Mekong Delta lined-pond survey (2021), 52.8% in the CTU-RAS model (2026) and 63.92% of variable costs in the Ca Mau above-ground tanks (2024).

### If FCR rises from 1.2 to 1.5, how much does the production cost increase?

Feed cost per kg of shrimp = FCR × feed price. At an assumed price of VND 30,000/kg, an FCR increase of 0.3 adds about VND 9,000 per kg of shrimp.

### Do I need to include liner depreciation in the production cost?

Yes. Depreciation accounts for about 8–14% of costs in the studies above.

### How can I tell whether a pond is making a profit or a loss before harvest?

Add up the costs spent so far, estimate the yield by cast-net sampling, calculate a provisional production cost and compare it with the current buying price.
