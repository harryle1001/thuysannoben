High-density whiteleg shrimp farming in lined ponds is now a popular model because it makes the bottom easier to control, reduces pathogens and shortens the culture period. However, high density means very large amounts of waste and high oxygen and mineral demand, requiring tighter management than earthen ponds.

## Lined pond design

- Round ponds or square ponds with rounded corners, commonly 500–2,000 m² in area.
- The bottom slopes towards the centre, with a **siphon pit** to collect waste.
- Paddlewheel aerators are arranged to create a rotating current that gathers waste towards the centre.
- Have a settling pond and a reservoir of treated water so water can be supplied whenever needed.

## Stocking density

Common densities range from 100–300 shrimp/m², depending on oxygen supply capacity, water treatment and experience. Newcomers should start at a moderate density and increase it gradually once they have mastered the procedure.

## Bottom and liner slime management

- **Siphon the bottom daily** or every other day from the third week.
- Liner slime forms from bacteria and organic matter adhering to the pond walls. Use liner-slime probiotics such as [CLEAR 12H](/san-pham/clear-12h) (helps clean liner slime; every 5–7 days per the label). Never apply probiotics on the same day as a disinfectant; wait 2–3 days after disinfection before reseeding probiotics.
- Bottom-treatment probiotics such as [AQUA BAC](/san-pham/aqua-bac) help break down the remaining waste.

## Mineral supplementation

Lined ponds receive no minerals from the soil, so shrimp easily become mineral-deficient and develop soft shell. Regularly apply minerals designed for lined ponds, such as [RIMIX](/san-pham/rimix), especially when shrimp are moulting; apply in the late afternoon or at night, when moulting peaks. Monitor alkalinity (high-density lined ponds are often kept at 150–200 mg/L CaCO3) and the Ca:Mg:K ratio.

## Oxygen and backup power

- High density requires many paddlewheel aerators and bottom aeration; keep dissolved oxygen above 5 mg/L (never below 4 mg/L), measured at dawn when it is lowest.
- A **backup generator** is essential. A power cut of 30–60 minutes at night can cause mass mortality.
- Keep oxygen tablets such as [SUPER OXY 02](/san-pham/super-oxy-02) in stock for emergencies; when shrimp surface gasping, run all aerators and stop feeding first, then apply the oxygen tablets.

## Water exchange

- Exchange 10–30% of the water regularly with treated water, increasing gradually as the shrimp grow.
- After water exchange, add minerals and probiotics to stabilise the environment.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do lined ponds need water colour to be built up?

Yes. Water colour helps stabilise pH and reduces stress for the shrimp. High-density lined ponds usually maintain a light water colour combined with probiotics.

### How often should a lined pond be siphoned?

From week 3 onwards, siphon daily; some ponds siphon twice a day once shrimp are large and feeding heavily.

### How is liner slime harmful to shrimp?

Liner slime harbours harmful bacteria, fouls the water, increases toxic gases and makes shrimp prone to intestinal disease and black gill. It needs to be cleaned regularly.
