The hepatopancreas is the shrimp's digestive and energy storage "factory". When it is healthy, shrimp feed vigorously, grow fast and tolerate environmental fluctuations. When the hepatopancreas becomes **atrophied, pale, white or swollen and waterlogged**, shrimp absorb feed poorly, grow slowly, are prone to gut diseases and may suffer prolonged scattered mortality. Checking the hepatopancreas regularly is something farmers should do every week.

## How to check the shrimp hepatopancreas

Catch 5–10 shrimp from the feeding trays or with a cast net and examine the cephalothorax against the light:

- **Healthy hepatopancreas**: yellowish brown to dark brown, evenly firm, with clear boundaries, taking up about one third of the head.
- **Pale or white hepatopancreas**: pale yellow or opaque white, soft and mushy when squeezed.
- **Atrophied hepatopancreas**: shrunken and pulled back towards the rear, leaving an empty space in the head.
- **Swollen, waterlogged hepatopancreas**: abnormally enlarged, watery, with uneven colour.

Shrimp with a weak hepatopancreas often also show an empty gut, loose faeces or white faeces, and progressively reduced feeding over successive meals.

## Causes of a weak hepatopancreas

### Vibrio bacteria and hepatopancreatic necrosis

Vibrio bacteria (especially *V. parahaemolyticus* strains carrying the pirA/pirB toxin genes that cause AHPND/EMS) attack the hepatopancreas, causing swelling, atrophy and necrosis; AHPND mostly occurs 10–35 days after stocking. See the article [acute hepatopancreatic necrosis disease (EMS)](/bai-viet/benh-hoai-tu-gan-tuy-cap-ems-tren-tom).

### EHP microsporidian

EHP parasitises the hepatopancreas, causing slow growth and size variation. See [EHP disease in shrimp](/bai-viet/benh-vi-bao-tu-trung-ehp-tren-tom).

### Toxins from the environment and feed

High toxic NH₃, NO₂ and H₂S, toxic algae, mouldy feed contaminated with mycotoxins and overuse of chemicals all overwork the hepatopancreas, and over time it becomes atrophied and pale.

### Overfeeding

Excess feed both fouls the pond bottom and overloads the hepatopancreas. Ponds fed beyond demand often have shrimp with an enlarged, pale hepatopancreas and long, loose faeces.

## What to do when shrimp have an atrophied or white hepatopancreas

### Step 1: Reduce the load on the hepatopancreas

- Reduce feed by 20–30% for 3–5 days, monitoring the feeding trays to adjust; increase aeration to keep dissolved oxygen ≥ 5 mg/L.
- Treat the pond bottom, absorb toxic gases, and stabilise pH and alkalinity. See [treating toxic gases in shrimp ponds](/bai-viet/xu-ly-khi-doc-nh3-no2-h2s-ao-nuoi-tom).

Check the hepatopancreas after 3–5 days. If mortality is still rising or the hepatopancreas is swollen and soft, send samples for testing or call a technician for a suitable treatment protocol.

## Preventing a weak hepatopancreas from the start of the crop

- Choose disease-free postlarvae that have tested PCR-negative for EHP and AHPND (EMS).
- Give liver tonics periodically for 3–5 days per course, repeating after a 5-day break, especially during the period from day 10 to day 45.
- Feed according to demand and store feed in a dry place.
- Keep the pond bottom clean and toxic gases low with regular probiotic applications.

## Frequently asked questions

### What colour is a healthy shrimp hepatopancreas?

A healthy hepatopancreas is yellowish brown to dark brown, evenly firm and clearly separated from the muscle. A pale, white, shrunken or swollen watery hepatopancreas is a sign that it is weakening.

### Can an atrophied hepatopancreas recover?

If detected early and caused by the environment or feed, the hepatopancreas can recover after 5–10 days of liver tonics and a reduced load. Atrophy caused by serious diseases such as EMS or EHP requires testing and treatment following a technician's guidance.

### How often should shrimp be given liver tonics?

Usually 3–5 days per course, a 5-day break and then repeat, continuously while shrimp are under 60 days of culture or when the weather is unstable.

### Does a swollen hepatopancreas mean the shrimp are eating a lot?

An enlarged, pale, watery hepatopancreas is usually a sign of an overloaded or infected hepatopancreas, not of healthy shrimp. Reduce feeding, treat the environment and give liver tonics.
